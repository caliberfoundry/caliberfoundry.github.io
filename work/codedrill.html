Option 1 · U.S. Customs Broker (CBLE) 19 CFR § 111.36(c): Freight-Forwarder Referral Compensation

A licensed U.S. customs broker agrees to compensate an unlicensed ocean freight forwarder for referring an importer’s consumption entry. The shipment was not purchased on a Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) basis and no written billing waiver exists. Under 19 CFR § 111.36(c), which condition is mandatory for this referral compensation to be lawful?

  1. A.The forwarder bundles customs brokerage into a single unitemized ocean freight invoice without disclosing the broker’s fee schedule to the importer.
  2. B.
    Correct · Verified by 19 CFR § 111.36(c)
    The broker notifies the importer in advance in writing of the compensated forwarder’s name, transmits a true copy of brokerage charges directly to the importer, and ensures the forwarder neither controls brokerage fees nor forbids direct broker-importer communication.
  3. C.The broker splits up to 50% of entry fees silently, provided CBP Form 7501 is filed within 10 working days of release.
  4. D.The unlicensed forwarder executes the Power of Attorney as sub-agent and routes all CBP inquiries exclusively through the forwarder’s desk.

Option 2 · IRS Enrolled Agent (SEE Part 3) 31 CFR § 10.22(a) & § 10.27: Circular 230 Diligence & Contingent Fees

An Enrolled Agent prepares an original corporate Form 1120 and later makes oral representations to an IRS Revenue Agent while defending an amended refund claim under examination. Under Treasury Circular 230 (31 CFR § 10.22 and § 10.27), which rule governs the practitioner’s accuracy duty and fee structure?

  1. A.Due diligence applies only to signed tax returns, and contingent fees are permitted on both original and amended returns.
  2. B.Oral representations to an IRS officer are exempt from Circular 230 due diligence unless submitted later by affidavit.
  3. C.
    Correct · Verified by 31 CFR § 10.22(a) & § 10.27(b)
    Due diligence applies to preparing returns and to both oral and written representations made to Treasury and clients (§ 10.22(a)(1)–(3)); contingent fees are prohibited for preparing an original tax return (§ 10.27(b)(1)) and allowed only in specific IRS examination, penalty/interest, or judicial proceedings (§ 10.27(b)(2)).
  4. D.Contingent fees are permitted on any original return reporting more than $10 million in gross receipts when Form 8275 is attached.

Option 3 · Customs Valuation (CBLE) 19 CFR § 152.103: Transaction Value & Foreign Mold Assists

A U.S. importer purchases 5,000 assemblies FOB Busan for $100,000, supplies foreign-manufactured injection molds free of charge ($12,000), pays a bona fide buying agent $3,000, and pays $6,500 for international ocean freight and marine insurance. Under 19 CFR § 152.103, what is the dutiable Transaction Value?

  1. A.
    Correct · Verified by 19 CFR § 152.103(b) & (d)
    $112,000: add the $12,000 foreign-produced mold assist to the $100,000 price actually paid or payable, while excluding the bona fide buying commission ($3,000) and international ocean freight/insurance ($6,500).
  2. B.$115,000: add both the $12,000 mold assist and the $3,000 buying agent commission.
  3. C.$118,500: add the $12,000 mold assist and the $6,500 international freight and insurance charges.
  4. D.$100,000: exclude the molds because they were furnished free of charge under a separate tooling contract.
