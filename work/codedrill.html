CBLE19 CFR § 111.36(c)(1)–(4)

A freight forwarder selects broker Vance to handle entries for Cascade Imports and will collect the brokerage charges. Cascade has given no written waiver. What must the parties do under 19 CFR § 111.36(c) for Vance to compensate the forwarder for this referral?

  1. A.A licensed customs broker is strictly prohibited under all circumstances from sharing fees or paying referral compensation to an unlicensed person or freight forwarder.
  2. B.Vance may compensate the freight forwarder provided she obtains prior written approval from the Center of Excellence and Expertise (CEE) Director and caps the referral fee at 10% of the brokerage charge.
  3. C.Vance may compensate the freight forwarder only if Cascade purchases the goods on a Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) basis and the forwarder acts as the Importer of Record.
  4. D.Correct · Give Cascade advance notice of the selected broker’s name. Vance must send her charges directly to Cascade, obtain the customs power of attorney directly from the importer of record, preserve direct communication, and comply with the other broker regulations.
  5. E.Vance may compensate the freight forwarder provided the forwarder rebates at least 50% of the referral commission back to the importer as a volume discount.

In addition to the invoice amount, Apex incurred the following costs not included in the seller's invoice price: • $4,000 paid to Apex's bona fide buying agent in Taipei for locating the factory and inspecting the goods; • $8,000 for a specialized aluminum injection mold purchased by Apex from an unrelated U.S. toolmaker and shipped free of charge to the Taiwanese manufacturer for use exclusively on this 5,000-unit order (plus $500 air freight to deliver the mold to the Taiwanese factory); • $6,000 for industrial design sketches created in Seattle by Apex's full-time U.S.-domiciled staff engineers and emailed free of charge to the Taiwanese factory; and • $3,500 in patent license royalties that the sales contract requires Apex to pay to a German patent holder as a condition of the sale of the trekking poles for exportation to the United States. What is the correct transaction value (appraised value) of the imported trekking poles under 19 CFR § 152.103?

  1. A.$102,500
  2. B.Correct · $114,500
  3. C.$118,500
  4. D.$120,500
  5. E.$127,000
