CBLE19 CFR § 111.36(c)(1)–(4)

A freight forwarder selects broker Vance to handle entries for Cascade Imports and will collect the brokerage charges. Cascade has given no written waiver. What must the parties do under 19 CFR § 111.36(c) for Vance to compensate the forwarder for this referral?

  1. A.A licensed customs broker is strictly prohibited under all circumstances from sharing fees or paying referral compensation to an unlicensed person or freight forwarder.
  2. B.Vance may compensate the freight forwarder provided she obtains prior written approval from the Center of Excellence and Expertise (CEE) Director and caps the referral fee at 10% of the brokerage charge.
  3. C.Vance may compensate the freight forwarder only if Cascade purchases the goods on a Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) basis and the forwarder acts as the Importer of Record.
  4. D.Correct · Give Cascade advance notice of the selected broker’s name. Vance must send her charges directly to Cascade, obtain the customs power of attorney directly from the importer of record, preserve direct communication, and comply with the other broker regulations.
  5. E.Vance may compensate the freight forwarder provided the forwarder rebates at least 50% of the referral commission back to the importer as a volume discount.

IRS SEE31 CFR § 10.20(a)(1)–(2) (Circular 230 — Information to be furnished)

[IRS Enrolled Agent Track — SEE Part 3] During an IRS examination of client Horizon Logistics Inc., an IRS Revenue Agent makes a proper and lawful written request to Enrolled Agent Thomas Greene for Horizon's warehouse inventory ledgers from two years ago. Greene does not have the inventory ledgers in his possession, and Horizon's current president informs Greene that the ledgers were retained by Horizon's former outside bookkeeper, Carla Moss, after her contract ended. None of the records are legally privileged. Under Treasury Department Circular 230 (31 CFR § 10.20), what are Enrolled Agent Greene's exact obligations regarding the Revenue Agent's request?

  • A. Greene must hire a private investigator to independently verify whether Carla Moss actually possesses the ledgers and subpoena Carla Moss before responding to the IRS.
  • B. Greene must promptly notify the requesting IRS officer that neither he nor his client possesses the records, provide any information Greene has regarding the identity of the person (Carla Moss) whom he believes may have possession or control of the requested records, and make reasonable inquiry of his client regarding the identity of any person who may have possession or control, without being required to make inquiry of any other person or independently verify the client's information.
  • C. Greene may refuse to respond to the IRS request entirely because the Enrollee-Client privilege under Circular 230 permanently shields all accounting ledgers from IRS examination.
  • D. Greene must reconstruct the missing inventory ledgers from memory and submit estimated figures to the IRS without disclosing that the original ledgers are held by Carla Moss.
  • E. Greene must immediately withdraw from representing Horizon Logistics Inc. whenever a requested document is not in the practitioner's physical office.

IRS SEEIRC § 2(b); 26 CFR § 1.2-2(b)(4), (c)(2)

Maya is unmarried at year-end and lives alone in a rental apartment in Seattle. Her widowed mother lives independently in a separate assisted-living apartment in Portland. During the entire tax year, Maya pays 70% of the cost of maintaining her mother’s apartment and properly claims her mother as a qualifying relative dependent. Maya has no other dependents. Under IRC § 2(b) and 26 CFR § 1.2-2, what is the most advantageous filing status Maya may claim?

  • A. Single, because a qualifying person must live in the taxpayer’s own home for more than half the year to claim Head of Household.
  • B. Head of Household, because a dependent parent does not have to live in the taxpayer’s own home when the taxpayer pays more than half the cost of maintaining the parent’s separate household for the entire year.
  • C. Qualifying Surviving Spouse, because Maya supports a widowed parent.
  • D. Head of Household only if Maya’s mother lives with Maya for at least 6 months during the calendar year.
  • E. Married Filing Separately, because supporting a parent in a separate residence triggers separate-household allocation.
