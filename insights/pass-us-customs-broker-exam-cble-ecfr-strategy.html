03 // Deterministic Valuation Formulas: Transaction Value, MPF, and HMF

Every CBLE includes calculation vignettes designed to penalize candidates who confuse CIF charges with statutory additions under 19 CFR § 152.103(b). Memorize and verify the exact deterministic equation below:

TRANSACTION VALUE (19 CFR § 152.103) = Price Actually Paid or Payable (PAPP — FOB Foreign Port) + Packing Costs incurred by buyer + Selling Commissions incurred by buyer + Statutory Assists (Tools, Dies, Molds, Foreign Engineering supplied free/reduced) + Royalties / License Fees paid as condition of sale + Proceeds of subsequent resale accruing to seller -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- EXCLUDED DEDUCTIONS (Do NOT add; subtract if included in CIF price): - International Ocean / Air Freight & Marine Insurance - Buying Agent Commissions (bona fide buying agency agreement) - Post-Importation Construction, Erection, Assembly, or Technical Assistance - U.S. Customs Duties and Federal Excise Taxes AD VALOREMUSER FEES: • Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF, Formal Entry): 0.3464% of Transaction Value (Subject to statutory minimum and maximum caps under 19 CFR § 24.23) • Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF, Ocean Commercial Port): 0.125% (0.00125) of Value
STATUTORY GOLDEN QUOTE // 19 CFR § 152.103(a)(1) “The price actually paid or payable will be considered without regard to its method of derivation. It may be the result of discounts, increases, or negotiations, or may be arrived at by the application of a formula... The word ‘payable’ refers to a situation in which the price has been agreed upon, but actual payment has not been made at the time of importation.”

04 // The 3-Step "Reverse-Option" Protocol for HTSUS Classification

Never read an HTSUS classification vignette and browse the tariff schedule from Chapter 1 to Chapter 99. The CBLE gives you five specific 10-digit HTSUS subheadings in options A through E. Execute the Reverse-Option Protocol:

  1. Diff the First 4 Digits (Heading Level) Across A–E: Identify whether the five options split across different Chapters (e.g., Chapter 39 Plastics vs. Chapter 85 Electrical Machinery) or within a single 4-digit Heading.
  2. Check Section and Chapter Notes First (GRI 1 Mandate): Under GRI 1, classification is determined legally by the terms of the headings and any relative Section or Chapter Notes. Over 60% of CBLE classification traps are decided by an Exclusion Note at the top of a Chapter (`This chapter does not cover...`).
  3. Compare Identical Indentation Levels Only (GRI 6): When choosing between subheadings (one-dash `-` vs. two-dash `--` vs. three-dash `---`), compare only subheadings at the exact same indentation depth before descending to the next level.

05 // Frequently Asked Questions (CBLE Preparation)

Why is the U.S. Customs Broker License Exam (CBLE) pass rate so low despite being open-book?
The CBLE requires answering 80 complex five-option (A–E) questions in 270 minutes (4.5 hours) with a 75% passing score (60/80 correct). That leaves 202.5 seconds per question. Candidates who study only static PDFs spend 150 to 240 seconds searching the electronic eCFR and HTSUS reference panels during the Pearson VUE exam, leaving insufficient time to analyze statutory exceptions.
Which Title 19 CFR Parts are most heavily tested on the Customs Broker License Exam?
Over 70% of non-classification CBLE questions draw from seven core regulatory parts: 19 CFR Part 111 (Customs Brokers), Part 113 (Customs Bonds & Liquidated Damages), Part 141 (Entry of Merchandise & Invoices), Part 142 (Entry Process & Release Deadlines), Part 152 (Classification & Appraisement / Transaction Value), Part 177 (Administrative Rulings), and Part 190 (Modernized Drawback under TFTEA).
