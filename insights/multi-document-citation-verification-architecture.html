01 // Why Naive Chunk-Based Vector RAG Fails Regulatory Audits

Most off-the-shelf document assistants slice PDFs and spreadsheets into arbitrary 500-to-1,000 token windows, embed them into a vector database, and retrieve top-k chunks by cosine similarity. While acceptable for casual knowledge bases, this pattern breaks down catastrophically in enterprise verification workflows for three structural reasons:

  • Table Header Decapitation: When a 40-row financial schedule or fee table is split across token boundaries, rows 25–40 lose their column headers (`FY2024 Audited`, `Pro Forma Adjusted`, `Currency: USD Thousands`) and footnote qualifiers.
  • Cross-Clause Exception Severance: In commercial leases, MSAs, and federal regulations, a baseline obligation on Page 14 (`Section 4.2: Tenant shall maintain coverage of $5,000,000`) is routinely modified by an addendum or schedule on Page 47 (`Schedule C: Notwithstanding Section 4.2...`). Pure semantic search retrieves the baseline obligation while missing the cross-referenced override.
  • Unverifiable Attribution: Returning a document filename (`Credit_Agreement_Final.pdf`) forces a human analyst to spend 15 minutes manually searching a 200-page PDF to confirm a single figure.

03 // The 4-Stage Deterministic Verification Pipeline

When an auditor, attorney, or operations lead submits a query across a corpus of hundreds of documents, OmniRAG™ executes a four-stage state machine rather than a single prompt call:

  1. Query Decomposition & Entity Extraction: Decomposes multi-part questions into atomic verification sub-queries, extracting exact statutory citations, contract clause numbers, date ranges, and financial line items.
  2. Hybrid Lexical + Structural Retrieval + Cross-Reference Expansion: Executes BM25 exact-token matching alongside semantic retrieval, then automatically follows internal cross-reference pointers (`as defined in Section 1.01`, `subject to Exhibit B`) so exceptions are pulled into the evidence context alongside primary clauses.
  3. Citation-Locked Synthesis: Every atomic claim generated by the synthesis layer must include an inline machine-verifiable citation pointer (`[REF: DOC-2026-MSA-04::P14::TBL02::R07::C03]`) and an exact verbatim quote span.
  4. Post-Generation Verification Gate: Before the response is rendered to the user, a deterministic validator checks every `[REF: ...]` tag against the retrieved AST nodes. If a number or verbatim quote does not appear in the referenced coordinate node, the claim is flagged or rejected.
