31 CFR Part 10 (Treasury Circular 230) Exam Guide: Key Rules & Traps for IRS Enrolled Agent Candidates

Part 3 of the IRS Special Enrollment Examination (SEE Part 3: Representation, Practices, and Procedures) heavily tests your precision on Treasury Department Circular No. 230 (codified at Title 31 CFR Part 10). Exam distractors routinely hinge on single words: whether a practitioner must advise the client versus notify the IRS, or whether records must be retained for 36 months versus 48 months.

01 // High-Frequency 31 CFR Part 10 Subpart B Statutory Matrix

Subpart B of Circular 230 (31 CFR §§ 10.20 through 10.38) governs the duties and restrictions relating to practice before the Internal Revenue Service. Below is the authoritative reference table of sections most frequently tested on SEE Part 3:

31 CFR Section Statutory Title Core Rule & Mandatory Threshold Primary Exam Distractor / Trap
§ 10.20 Information to be Furnished Must promptly submit non-privileged records/information upon a proper and lawful request by a duly authorized IRS officer. If the practitioner lacks possession, they must promptly notify the IRS officer and provide the identity of the person they believe has possession (no duty to conduct an independent investigation).
§ 10.21 Knowledge of Client’s Omission Promptly advise the client of the fact of noncompliance, error, or omission and its consequences under the IRC. Trap: Exam options stating the practitioner must report the client to the IRS or unilaterally file an amended return are always incorrect.
§ 10.22 Diligence as to Accuracy Must exercise due diligence in preparing/assisting in returns, documents, and oral/written representations to Treasury or clients. A practitioner may rely on the work product of another person only if the practitioner used reasonable care in engaging, supervising, training, and evaluating that person.
§ 10.27 Fees (Contingent Fees) A practitioner may not charge an unconscionable fee, and may not charge a contingent fee for preparing an original tax return. Contingent fees are permitted only for specific IRS examination/challenge of an original return, claims for credit/refund of statutory interest/penalties, or judicial proceedings.
§ 10.28 Return of Client’s Records Must promptly return all Records of the Client necessary for the client to comply with federal tax obligations, even if there is a fee dispute. If applicable state law permits a lien on records for unpaid fees, the practitioner may withhold only their own work product—never the client's original source documents (W-2s, 1099s, canceled checks).
§ 10.29 Conflicting Interests May represent conflicting interests only if the practitioner reasonably believes they can provide competent/diligent representation, it is not prohibited by law, and each affected client gives informed consent confirmed in writing. Written consent must be confirmed within 30 days of oral consent and retained for at least 36 months after the conclusion of the representation.
§ 10.30 Solicitation & Advertising Enrolled Agents may not use the term “certified” or imply an employer/employee relationship with the IRS. Copies of direct mail and e-mail fee schedules/solicitations must be retained for at least 36 months from the date of the last transmission or use.
§ 10.34 Standards With Respect to Tax Returns & Documents May not sign a tax return or advise a position that lacks a reasonable basis, or an unreasonable position (IRC § 6694) unless disclosed on Form 8275 / 8275-R (or substantial authority exists for undisclosed positions). Tax shelters and reportable transactions require the higher more likely than not (>50%) confidence standard.

02 // Exact Statutory Text Analysis: The Three Most Tested Sections

31 CFR § 10.21 // KNOWLEDGE OF CLIENT’S OMISSION “A practitioner who, having been retained by a client with respect to a matter administered by the Internal Revenue Service, knows that the client has not complied with the revenue laws of the United States or has made an error in or omission from any return, document, affidavit, or other paper which the client submitted or executed under the revenue laws of the United States, must advise the client promptly of the fact of such noncompliance, error, or omission. The practitioner must advise the client of the consequences as provided under the Code and regulations of such noncompliance, error, or omission.”
31 CFR § 10.29(b)(3) // CONFLICTING INTERESTS — 36-MONTH RETENTION “Each affected client waives the conflict of interest and gives informed consent, confirmed in writing by each affected client, at the time the existence of the conflict of interest is known by the practitioner. The confirmation may be made within a reasonable period after the informed consent, but in no event later than 30 days. Copies of the written consents must be retained by the practitioner for at least 36 months from the date of the conclusion of the representation of the affected clients...”

03 // Enrollment Renewal Cycle & Continuing Education (31 CFR § 10.6)

Memorize the numerical parameters under 31 CFR § 10.6 for Enrolled Agent active renewal:

  • Renewal Cycle: Every 3 years, staggered by the last digit of the practitioner's Social Security Number or tax identification number (using IRS Form 8554).
  • Total Continuing Education (CE) Hours: Minimum of 72 hours per 3-year enrollment cycle.
  • Annual Minimums: At least 16 hours per enrollment year, which must include at least 2 hours of Ethics or Professional Conduct every year.
  • CE Record Retention: Practitioners must retain CE completion certificates for 4 years following the date of renewal of enrollment.

04 // Frequently Asked Questions (31 CFR Part 10 / Circular 230)

What must a practitioner do under Circular 230 § 10.21 upon discovering an error or omission on a client's prior tax return?
Under 31 CFR § 10.21, the practitioner must promptly advise the client of the fact of such noncompliance, error, or omission and advise the client of the consequences under the Internal Revenue Code and regulations. Circular 230 does not authorize or require the practitioner to notify the IRS or file an amended return without client authorization.
How long must a practitioner retain conflict-of-interest written consents under 31 CFR § 10.29?
Under 31 CFR § 10.29(b)(3), written consent must be confirmed within 30 days of informed consent and retained by the practitioner for at least 36 months from the date of the conclusion of the representation of the affected clients.
Can a practitioner withhold a client's original tax records if the client has not paid the practitioner's fee (§ 10.28)?
No. Under 31 CFR § 10.28(a), even in the presence of a fee dispute, the practitioner must promptly return all original client records necessary for the client to comply with federal tax obligations.
