В§ 177.9 Effect of ruling letters.

(a) Effect of ruling letters generally. A ruling letter issued by Customs and Border Protection under the provisions of this part represents the official position of Customs and Border Protection with respect to the particular transaction or issue described therein and is binding on all Customs and Border Protection personnel in accordance with the provisions of this section until modified or revoked. In the absence of a change of practice or other modification or revocation which affects the principle of the ruling set forth in the ruling letter, that principle may be cited as authority in the disposition of transactions involving the same circumstances. Generally, a ruling letter is effective on the date it is issued and may be applied to all entries which are unliquidated, or other transactions with respect to which Customs and Border Protection has not taken final action on that date.

[03] Frequently Tested CBLE Questions on 19 CFR В§ 177.9 & В§ 177.12

[Q-01] When does a newly issued CBP binding ruling letter take effect under 19 CFR 177.9?

Under 19 CFR 177.9(a), an initial binding ruling letter is generally effective immediately on the date it is issued and may be applied to all unliquidated entries or pending transactions involving identical merchandise and circumstances.

[Q-02] How much notice must CBP give when modifying or revoking an existing binding ruling under 19 CFR 177.12?

For any interpretive ruling in effect for 60 or more calendar days, 19 CFR 177.12 requires CBP to publish a proposed notice in the Customs Bulletin with a 30-calendar-day public comment period, followed by a final notice in the Customs Bulletin that becomes effective 60 calendar days after publication.

[Q-03] Can you request a prospective binding ruling under 19 CFR 177.1 for goods that have already been entered?

No. Prospective binding rulings under 19 CFR 177.1 only apply to prospective transactions (merchandise not yet imported). Once an entry is pending before a CBP port or Center, the correct mechanism is a request for Internal Advice under 19 CFR 177.11.
