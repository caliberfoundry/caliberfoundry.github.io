HTSUS General Rule of Interpretation 3(b): Essential Character & Retail Sets

How to distinguish a true GRI 3(b) retail set from separately classifiable items — and when to fall back to GRI 3(c) "Last in Numerical Order".

[FAST-FIND] Pearson VUE eCFR Navigation Path: Open HTSUS General Notes & General Rules of Interpretation (GRI 1-6) → Query: "3(b)" OR "essential character" OR "sets for retail sale"
[TACTICAL NOTE] Always check if all items in the box work together for ONE specific activity. If a food item and a permanent tool or mismatched items do not meet a single specific need, they are NOT a GRI 3(b) set and must be classified separately.

§ [01] Verbatim Statutory Text & Golden Quotes (HTSUS GRI 3(b))

Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS) // General Rules of Interpretation // Rule 3
General Rule of Interpretation 3 (GRI 3)

When, by application of rule 2(b) or for any other reason, goods are, prima facie, classifiable under two or more headings, classification shall be effected as follows:

(a) Relative Specificity: The heading which provides the most specific description shall be preferred to headings providing a more general description. However, when two or more headings each refer to part only of the materials or substances contained in mixed or composite goods or to part only of the items in a set put up for retail sale, those headings are to be regarded as equally specific in relation to those goods, even if one of them gives a more complete or precise description of the goods.

(b) Essential Character: Mixtures, composite goods consisting of different materials or made up of different components, and goods put up in sets for retail sale, which cannot be classified by reference to 3(a), shall be classified as if they consisted of the material or component which gives them their essential character, insofar as this criterion is applicable.

(c) Last in Numerical Order: When goods cannot be classified by reference to 3(a) or 3(b), they shall be classified under the heading which occurs last in numerical order among those which equally merit consideration.

Explanatory Note X to GRI 3(b) — Mandatory 3-Part Test for "Goods Put Up in Sets for Retail Sale"

For the purposes of this Rule, the term "goods put up in sets for retail sale" shall be taken to mean goods which:
[01] Consist of at least two different articles which are, prima facie, classifiable in different headings;
[02] Consist of products or articles put up together to meet a particular need or carry out a specific activity; and
[03] Are put up in a manner suitable for sale directly to end users without repacking (e.g., in boxes or cases or on boards).

[02] CBP Exam Trap Analysis — Socratic Code-Path Breakdown

[3 DISTRACTOR VECTORS]
[TRAP-01] — The "Fake Set" (Failing Requirement #2: Specific Activity)
[DISTRACTOR PATH] Candidates see multiple items packaged together in a shrink-wrapped gift basket (e.g., a bottle of wine, a wristwatch, and a plush toy) and immediately apply GRI 3(b) to classify everything under the highest-value item.
[STATUTORY RULE — HTSUS GRI 3(b)] If the items do not work together to carry out ONE specific activity (like making spaghetti or grooming hair), the package FAILS the GRI 3(b) retail set test. Every article must be classified separately under GRI 1.
[TRAP-02] — Applying GRI 3(a) Specificity to a Retail Set
[DISTRACTOR PATH] Candidates try to pick the component with the longest or most detailed tariff heading description under GRI 3(a).
[STATUTORY RULE — HTSUS GRI 3(b)] Look closely at the second sentence of GRI 3(a): when two or more headings each refer to "part only of the items in a set put up for retail sale, those headings are to be regarded as equally specific." Therefore, you MUST skip GRI 3(a) and move directly to GRI 3(b).
[TRAP-03] — Equally Split Essential Character -> GRI 3(c)
[DISTRACTOR PATH] When a composite good (such as a 50% reversible jacket or a dual-function pen/flashlight where neither predominates) has no clear essential character, candidates pick the lowest duty rate or first heading.
[STATUTORY RULE — HTSUS GRI 3(b)] When neither component imparts essential character under GRI 3(b), GRI 3(c) mandates classifying the good under the heading that occurs LAST in numerical order among those that equally merit consideration.

[03] Frequently Tested CBLE Questions on HTSUS GRI 3(b)

[Q-01] What are the 3 requirements for a retail set under HTSUS GRI 3(b)?

To qualify as "goods put up in sets for retail sale" under GRI 3(b), the package must: (1) consist of at least two different articles prima facie classifiable in different headings, (2) be put up together to meet a particular need or carry out a specific activity, and (3) be packaged ready for direct sale to end users without repacking.

[Q-02] Does the highest dollar value always determine essential character under GRI 3(b)?

No. While value is one factor, essential character may be determined by the nature of the material or component, its bulk, quantity, weight, value, or the role of a constituent material in relation to the use of the goods.

[Q-03] When do you use GRI 3(c) instead of GRI 3(b) on the CBLE?

You only use GRI 3(c) ("heading which occurs last in numerical order") when the goods qualify as a mixture, composite good, or retail set, AND no single material or component imparts the essential character under GRI 3(b).

An importer enters a shrink-wrapped "Home Pasta Dinner Kit" packaged ready for direct retail sale without repacking. The box contains: (1) a packet of uncooked dry spaghetti (Heading 1902, value $2.50), (2) a glass jar of tomato pasta sauce (Heading 2103, value $2.00), (3) a small packet of grated Parmesan cheese (Heading 0406, value $1.25), and (4) a novelty stainless steel corkscrew for opening wine bottles (Heading 8205, value $6.00). How is this importation classified under the General Rules of Interpretation?
Select one best answer (A–E): Click [STRIKE] to cross out distractors
§ Official HTSUS GRI 3(b) Citation Breakdown:
Under GRI 3(b) and Explanatory Note X, articles put up in a set for retail sale must work together to meet a particular need or carry out a single specific activity (here, preparing a pasta meal). The dry spaghetti, pasta sauce, and grated Parmesan work together to make a pasta dish, with the spaghetti (Heading 1902) imparting the essential character of the pasta dinner kit. However, the wine corkscrew does not contribute to preparing the pasta meal — it is an extraneous item for a different activity (opening wine) and must be classified separately under Heading 8205.
[DISTRACTOR LEDGER — OPTIONS A–E]:
[A] Trap. Even though the corkscrew has the highest value ($6.00), it does not participate in the specific activity of preparing the pasta meal.
[B] GRI 3(c) only applies when goods qualify as a set/composite good under 3(b) AND essential character cannot be determined.
[C] VERIFIED ANSWER. The pasta, sauce, and cheese meet the specific-activity test for a pasta meal (essential character: Heading 1902), whereas the extraneous corkscrew is classified separately under Heading 8205.
[D] GRI 3(a) explicitly states that headings referring to only part of a retail set are regarded as equally specific.
[E] Food products that are cooked/prepared together to create a specific dish (like a spaghetti meal kit) DO qualify as a GRI 3(b) set.
