Title 19 вЂ” Customs Duties // Chapter I вЂ” U.S. Customs and Border Protection // Part 141 вЂ” Entry of Merchandise // Subpart F вЂ” Invoices
В§ 141.86 Contents of invoices and general requirements.

(a) General information required on the invoice. Each invoice of imported merchandise, must set forth the following information:
(1) The port of entry to which the merchandise is destined;
(2) The time when, the place where, and the person by whom and the person to whom the merchandise is sold or agreed to be sold...;
(3) A detailed description of the merchandise, including the name by which each item is known, the grade or quality, and the marks, numbers, and symbols under which sold by the seller or manufacturer to the trade in the country of exportation, together with the marks and numbers of the packages in which the merchandise is packed;
(4) The quantities in the weights and measures of the country or place from which the merchandise is shipped, or in the weights and measures of the United States;
(5) The purchase price of each item in the currency of the purchase, if the merchandise is shipped in pursuance of a purchase or an agreement to purchase;
(6) If the merchandise is shipped otherwise than in pursuance of a purchase or an agreement to purchase, the value for each item, in the currency in which the transactions are usually made...;
(7) The kind of currency, whether gold, silver, or paper;
(8) All charges upon the merchandise itemized by name and amount, including freight, insurance, commission, cases, containers, coverings, and cost of packing; and if not included above, all charges, costs, and expenses incurred in bringing the merchandise from alongside the carrier at the port of exportation in the country of exportation and placing it alongside the carrier at the first United States port of entry...;
(9) All rebates, drawbacks, and bounties, separately itemized, allowed upon the exportation of the merchandise;
(10) The country of origin of the merchandise; and,
(11) All goods or services furnished for the production of the merchandise (e.g., assists such as dies, molds, tools, engineering work) not included in the invoice price.

(d) Language required. The invoice and all attachments must be in the English language, or must have attached thereto an accurate translation in the English language, signed by the translator or other person having knowledge of the truth of the translation.

[03] Frequently Tested CBLE Questions on 19 CFR В§ 141.86 & В§ 141.89

[Q-01] What are the 11 mandatory items on a commercial invoice under 19 CFR 141.86(a)?

19 CFR 141.86(a)(1)-(11) requires: (1) destination port of entry, (2) time/place/buyer/seller of sale, (3) detailed description with trade marks/numbers and package marks, (4) quantities in weights/measures, (5) purchase price per item in currency of purchase, (6) value if not purchased, (7) kind of currency (gold, silver, or paper), (8) itemized charges (freight, insurance, commission, packing), (9) itemized export rebates/drawbacks/bounties, (10) country of origin, and (11) assists not included in invoice price.

[Q-02] What if a foreign commercial invoice is written in Mandarin, Spanish, or German?

Under 19 CFR 141.86(d), the invoice and all attachments must either be in the English language or have attached an accurate English translation signed by the translator or another person having knowledge of the truth of the translation.

[Q-03] How long does an importer have to produce a missing commercial invoice when entering on a pro forma invoice?

Under 19 CFR 141.91(d) and 113.42, when entry is made on a pro forma invoice, the importer gives a bond condition to produce the required commercial invoice within 120 days from the date of entry (or 50 days if the invoice is needed solely for statistical purposes).
