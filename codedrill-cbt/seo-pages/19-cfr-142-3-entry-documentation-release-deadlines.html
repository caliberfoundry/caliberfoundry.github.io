19 CFR § 142.3 & § 142.12: Entry Documentation & The 10-Working-Day Summary Rule

Never confuse "15 Calendar Days to File Entry (142.2)" with "10 Working Days to File Entry Summary 7501 (142.12)" on the CBLE.

[FAST-FIND] Pearson VUE eCFR Navigation Path: Open 19 CFR Part 142 (Entry Process) -> Subparts A & B → Query: "142.3" OR "142.12" OR "10 working days"
[TACTICAL NOTE] Pin this dual deadline table in your mind: § 142.2 Entry (CBP Form 3461) = within 15 CALENDAR days after arrival (or goods go to General Order warehouse). § 142.12 Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501 + Duty) = within 10 WORKING days after time of entry/release.

§ 142.2 Time for filing entry.

(a) General. Entry documentation required by § 142.3 must be filed within 15 calendar days after the date of arrival of a commercial conveyance in the United States, unless an extension is granted... If entry is not made within the 15-calendar-day period, the merchandise will be placed in a General Order (G.O.) warehouse at the risk and expense of the consignee.

§ 142.3 Entry documentation required.

(a) Contents. Except as provided in paragraph (b) of this section, the entry documentation required to secure the release of merchandise must consist of the following:
(1) Entry (CBP Form 3461, or its electronic equivalent, or CBP Form 7533 for immediate delivery);
(2) Evidence of the right to make entry (e.g., bill of lading, air waybill, carrier’s certificate under Part 141);
(3) A commercial invoice, or a pro forma invoice when the commercial invoice cannot be produced;
(4) A packing list, where appropriate;
(5) Other documents necessary to determine merchandise admissibility; and
(6) Identification of the importer of record and consignee on the entry form.

§ 142.12 Time for filing or submission of entry summary documentation.

(b) When entry summary is not filed at time of entry. When the entry documentation is filed before the entry summary documentation, the importer must file the entry summary documentation within 10 working days after the time of entry (together with estimated duties attached, per § 142.13).

[3 DISTRACTOR VECTORS]
[TRAP-01] — 15 Calendar Days (§ 142.2) vs. 10 Working Days (§ 142.12)
[DISTRACTOR PATH] CBP gives a calendar puzzle with weekends and Federal holidays, asking for the Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501) due date, and provides a distractor calculated using 10 calendar days or 15 working days.
[STATUTORY RULE — 19 CFR § 142.3 & § 142.12] Initial Entry for release (§ 142.2) is 15 CALENDAR days after arrival. Follow-up Entry Summary CBP Form 7501 with estimated duties (§ 142.12(b)) is 10 WORKING days (excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and Federal holidays) after the time of entry/release.
[TRAP-02] — Quota Merchandise & Live Entry Exceptions (§ 142.13)
[DISTRACTOR PATH] Candidates assume the importer ALWAYS gets 10 working days after release to pay estimated duties and file the Entry Summary.
[STATUTORY RULE — 19 CFR § 142.3 & § 142.12] Under § 142.12(a) and § 142.13, if the importer is delinquent on bills, or if the merchandise is subject to a tariff-rate or absolute QUOTA (or special CBP directive), Entry Summary (7501) + estimated duties must be filed AT THE TIME OF ENTRY ("Live Entry") before release.
[TRAP-03] — Missing Commercial Invoice -> Pro Forma Invoice Bond Period (§ 141.91 / § 113.42)
[DISTRACTOR PATH] Candidates think merchandise cannot be released under § 142.3 without the original final commercial invoice.
[STATUTORY RULE — 19 CFR § 142.3 & § 142.12] Under § 142.3(a)(3) and § 141.91, a pro forma invoice may be accepted at entry if the importer gives a bond stipulation to produce the final commercial invoice within 120 days from the date of entry (50 days for statistical-only invoices).

[03] Frequently Tested CBLE Questions on 19 CFR § 142.3 & § 142.12

[Q-01] What is the difference between the 15-day rule in 19 CFR 142.2 and the 10-day rule in 19 CFR 142.12?

19 CFR 142.2 requires initial Entry documentation (CBP Form 3461) to be filed within 15 CALENDAR days after conveyance arrival to prevent goods from going to a General Order (G.O.) warehouse. Once goods are entered/released, 19 CFR 142.12(b) requires the Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501) and estimated duties to be filed within 10 WORKING days after the time of entry.

[Q-02] What 6 items comprise the required entry documentation under 19 CFR 142.3(a)?

The 6 items under 19 CFR 142.3(a) are: (1) CBP Form 3461/7533 or electronic equivalent, (2) evidence of the right to make entry, (3) commercial invoice (or pro forma invoice), (4) packing list where appropriate, (5) other admissibility documents required by PGAs/CBP, and (6) importer and consignee identification.

[Q-03] What happens if an importer fails to file Entry Summary within 10 working days?

Failure to timely file Entry Summary within 10 working days breaches the Basic Importation Bond (19 CFR 113.62), triggers liquidated damages, and allows the Port Director/CBP under 19 CFR 142.13 to demand "Live Entry" (requiring future entry summaries and duties at the time of entry before release).

§ Official 19 CFR § 142.3 & § 142.12 Citation Breakdown:
Under 19 CFR § 142.12(b), the Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501) must be filed within 10 WORKING days after the Time of Entry. Here, Time of Entry (release) is Wednesday, May 6. Count 10 working days (skipping Saturdays and Sundays) starting the next working day: - Days 1–2: Thu May 7, Fri May 8 - (Skip Sat May 9, Sun May 10) - Days 3–7: Mon May 11, Tue May 12, Wed May 13, Thu May 14, Fri May 15 - (Skip Sat May 16, Sun May 17) - Days 8–10: Mon May 18, Tue May 19, Wed May 20. Therefore, Wednesday, May 20 is the 10th working day.
[DISTRACTOR LEDGER — OPTIONS A–E]:
[A] Counts 10 calendar days instead of 10 working days.
[B] Applies the 15-calendar-day rule from § 142.2 (which governs initial entry after arrival to avoid General Order warehouse, not Entry Summary).
[C] VERIFIED ANSWER. 10 working days after Time of Entry (Wednesday, May 6) lands on Wednesday, May 20.
[D] Counts from the date CBP Form 3461 was transmitted (May 5) rather than the Time of Entry/release (May 6) per § 141.68 and § 142.12(b).
[E] Off-by-one counting error.
