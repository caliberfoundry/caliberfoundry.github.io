§ 152.103 Transaction value.

(a) Price actually paid or payable — (1) General. In determining transaction value, the price actually paid or payable will be considered without regard to its method of derivation. It may be the result of discounts, increases, or negotiations, or may be arrived at by the application of a formula... The price actually paid or payable is the total payment (whether direct or indirect, and exclusive of any costs, charges, or expenses incurred for transportation, insurance, and related services incident to the international shipment of the merchandise from the country of exportation to the place of importation in the United States) made, or to be made, for imported merchandise by the buyer to, or for the benefit of, the seller.

(b) Additions to price actually paid or payable. (1) The transaction value of imported merchandise is the price actually paid or payable for the merchandise when sold for exportation to the United States, plus amounts equal to:
(i) The packing costs incurred by the buyer with respect to the imported merchandise;
(ii) Any selling commission incurred by the buyer with respect to the imported merchandise;
(iii) The value, apportioned as appropriate, of any assist;
(iv) Any royalty or license fee related to the imported merchandise that the buyer is required to pay, directly or indirectly, as a condition of the sale of the imported merchandise for exportation to the United States; and
(v) The proceeds of any subsequent resale, disposal, or use of the imported merchandise that accrue, directly or indirectly, to the seller.

§ 152.102(a)(1)(iv) — The U.S. Design & Engineering Assist Exclusion

Assist includes: (iv) Engineering, development, artwork, design work, and plans and sketches that are undertaken elsewhere than in the United States and are necessary for the production of the imported merchandise.
(2) Exceptions: The specified work will NOT be treated as an assist if it is undertaken within the United States.

[02] CBP Exam Trap Analysis — Socratic Code-Path Breakdown

[3 DISTRACTOR VECTORS]
[TRAP-01] — Engineering / Artwork Performed Inside vs. Outside the U.S.
[DISTRACTOR PATH] Candidates add ALL free design, engineering, or CAD blueprints supplied by the buyer to the foreign factory as an assist.
[STATUTORY RULE — 19 CFR § 152.103] Under 19 CFR § 152.102(a)(1)(iv), engineering, development, artwork, design work, and plans/sketches are ONLY dutiable assists if undertaken ELSEWHERE than in the United States. However, physical molds, tools, dies, and raw materials ARE dutiable assists even if produced in the United States (plus freight to send the mold to the foreign factory).
[TRAP-02] — Buying Commissions vs. Selling Commissions
[DISTRACTOR PATH] Candidates add all agent commissions to Transaction Value.
[STATUTORY RULE — 19 CFR § 152.103] Only SELLING commissions incurred by the buyer (paid to the seller’s agent) are added under § 152.103(b)(1)(ii). Bona fide BUYING commissions (paid to the buyer’s own agent for representing the buyer abroad) are NON-dutiable.
[TRAP-03] — International Freight, Marine Insurance & Post-Importation Assembly
[DISTRACTOR PATH] When a CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) invoice includes itemized ocean freight and marine insurance, candidates leave them inside Transaction Value.
[STATUTORY RULE — 19 CFR § 152.103] Under § 152.103(a)(1) and § 152.103(i), international freight, marine insurance, and post-importation construction/assembly/maintenance in the U.S. (if separately identified from the PAPP) are DEDUCTED/EXCLUDED from Transaction Value.

[03] Frequently Tested CBLE Questions on 19 CFR § 152.103

[Q-01] What are the 5 statutory additions to Transaction Value under 19 CFR 152.103(b)?

The 5 statutory additions (to the extent not already included in the price actually paid or payable) are: (1) packing costs incurred by the buyer, (2) selling commissions incurred by the buyer, (3) apportioned value of any assist, (4) royalties or license fees paid as a condition of the sale, and (5) proceeds of any subsequent resale/disposal/use that accrue to the seller.

[Q-02] Is U.S. engineering or design work considered a dutiable assist?

No. Under 19 CFR 152.102(a)(1)(iv), engineering, development, artwork, design work, and plans/sketches are only dutiable assists if undertaken outside the United States. However, physical tools, dies, molds, and materials ARE dutiable assists even if manufactured in the United States.

[Q-03] Does the value of an assist include the shipping cost to send the assist to the foreign factory?

Yes. Under 19 CFR 152.103(d), the value of an assist includes the cost of transporting the assist to the foreign place of production.

Start with the FOB Price Actually Paid or Payable (PAPP) = $50,000. [01] Export packing incurred by buyer (+$3,000): Dutiable statutory addition under 19 CFR § 152.103(b)(1)(i). [02] Buying agent commission ($2,500): NON-dutiable (only selling commissions are added). [03] Steel injection mold (+$4,000) + transportation of the assist to the foreign factory (+$500) = +$4,500: Physical tools/dies/molds ARE dutiable assists under § 152.102(a)(1)(ii) regardless of where made, and § 152.103(d)(1) includes the cost of transporting the assist to the place of production. [04] CAD engineering developed in Chicago ($6,000): NON-dutiable under § 152.102(a)(1)(iv) because engineering undertaken within the United States is excluded. [05] Ocean freight & marine insurance ($3,500): Excluded under § 152.103(a)(1). Total Transaction Value = $50,000 + $3,000 + $4,500 = $57,500.
