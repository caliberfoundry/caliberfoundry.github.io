Three Strategic Facts About the DOJ ADA Title II Rule for Higher Ed & Vendors

  • Password-Protected LMS Courses Are Covered: Unlike older misconceptions, the 2024/2026 DOJ rule covers password-protected Canvas/Moodle/Blackboard course materials used in active classes—not just public marketing pages.
  • Third-Party Vendor Content Counts: Public universities remain legally responsible when contracted EdTech platforms or publisher labs fail WCAG 2.1 AA, driving strict VPAT 2.5 gates in RFPs.
  • Archived Course Exception Has 4 Strict Conditions: Legacy courses only qualify for the archive exception if created before the compliance date, kept solely for reference, stored in a dedicated archive area, and unaltered since archiving.

Technical Drill-Down 01 — Five Narrow Exceptions in 28 CFR § 35.201
  1. Archived web content (all 4 archival conditions met and never edited).
  2. Preexisting conventional electronic documents (PDF/Word/PPT posted before compliance date unless currently used to apply for, access, or participate in services/courses).
  3. Content posted by unaffiliated third parties (not under contractual/licensing arrangement).
  4. Individualized, password-protected conventional documents about a specific person (e.g., a single student's PDF financial statement—still subject to on-request accessible format duties).
  5. Preexisting social media posts published prior to the compliance deadline.
